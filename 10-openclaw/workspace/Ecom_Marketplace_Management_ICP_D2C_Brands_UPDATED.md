# 1. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

## 1.1 ICP Statement

Aneeverse’s ideal customer is a growing Indian D2C product brand that has ready products, clear category focus, and ambition to grow marketplace revenue through platforms such as Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, Ajio, Nykaa, Tata Cliq, Meesho, or other relevant marketplaces.

The best-fit brand is not looking for only basic catalogue upload work. They need a marketplace growth partner who can manage listing optimization, marketplace operations, account health, campaign coordination, offer planning, reporting, and monthly execution.

---

## 1.2 Defined Customer Avatar

### Customer Avatar: Founder-Led D2C Brand Owner

**Name:** Representative avatar — Founder / Co-founder / Ecommerce Head  
**Business Type:** D2C product brand  
**Location:** India-focused, with ability to sell pan-India through marketplaces  
**Business Stage:** Early-growth to scaling stage  
**Team Size:** 3–50 people  
**Catalogue Size:** 20–500 SKUs  
**Monthly Revenue Range:** ₹5 lakh to ₹2 crore+ overall business revenue  
**Current Marketplace Revenue:** ₹0 to ₹50 lakh/month, with serious growth ambition  
**Primary Goal:** Increase marketplace sales without hiring a full internal marketplace team  
**Primary Buyer:** Founder, co-founder, ecommerce manager, growth manager, marketplace head  

### What They Want

- More sales from Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, Ajio, Nykaa, Tata Cliq, or similar platforms
- Better product visibility and ranking
- Higher conversion rate from marketplace traffic
- Better catalogue presentation
- Cleaner marketplace operations
- Lower dependency on internal team bandwidth
- Monthly reporting and clear accountability

### What They Are Struggling With

- Products are listed but not selling well
- Listings are weak, incomplete, or not SEO-optimized
- Marketplace ads are spending money without clear returns
- Founder does not know what is working and what is not
- Competitors appear stronger on marketplaces
- Returns, reviews, account health, and inventory create operational stress
- No structured monthly marketplace growth plan exists

## 1.3 Best-Fit Categories

Aneeverse should prioritize D2C categories where product presentation, trust, pricing, reviews, catalogue quality, and marketplace visibility directly influence sales.

### High-Priority Categories

- Jewellery and fashion accessories
- Luggage, bags, backpacks, and travel accessories
- Beauty and personal care
- Apparel and ethnic wear
- Footwear and accessories
- Home decor and lifestyle products
- Premium gifting products
- Kitchen, home utility, and consumer lifestyle products
- Wellness and personal care products

### Strongest Proof Categories from Current Experience

#### Jewellery Brands

Jewellery is a trust-heavy and visual category. Buyers care about design, material, plating, skin safety, packaging, authenticity, return policy, and reviews. Marketplace success depends heavily on image sequencing, keyword-rich listings, trust-building details, and strong product presentation.

#### Luggage and Travel Goods Brands

Luggage is a feature-led category. Buyers compare size, compartments, wheels, locks, material, warranty, weight, and durability. Marketplace listings must make these details clear and easy to compare.

## 1.5 Negative ICP: Poor-Fit Customers

Aneeverse should avoid brands that are unlikely to succeed even with good execution.

### Avoid These Customers

- Idea-stage businesses with no ready products
- Brands with very few SKUs and no clear hero product
- Sellers who only want cheap product upload work
- Brands unwilling to improve images, content, pricing, or operations
- Businesses expecting guaranteed sales immediately
- Companies with no GST/documents required for marketplace onboarding
- Brands with poor product quality and high return risk
- Founders who delay approvals and do not share performance data
- Businesses unwilling to invest for at least 3 months

### Red Flags

- “Can you guarantee sales in month one?”
- “We do not want to spend anything on ads.”
- “Just upload products, nothing else.”
- “We want premium results at the cheapest price.”
- “We do not have product photos, inventory discipline, or documents.”

# 2. Insights from Customer Conversations / Interviews Conducted

## 2.1 Current Status

Formal structured customer interviews have **not yet been documented** in this file. The current insights are based on Aneeverse’s working experience with jewellery and luggage categories, marketplace execution observations, and common pain points seen in D2C marketplace management.

For a stronger investor/client-facing version, Aneeverse should conduct and document at least **8–12 discovery conversations** with D2C founders or ecommerce managers.

---

## 2.2 Current Conversation-Derived Insights

### Insight 1: Brands usually underestimate marketplace execution complexity

Many D2C founders think marketplace growth is mainly about uploading products. In reality, performance depends on catalogue quality, keyword optimization, pricing, reviews, inventory, ads, account health, and offer timing.

**Business implication:** Aneeverse should sell marketplace management as a monthly growth and operations function, not as a one-time listing upload service.

---

### Insight 2: Catalogue quality directly affects conversion

In categories like jewellery and luggage, buyers rely heavily on images, specifications, titles, bullets, reviews, and trust signals. Weak product pages reduce conversion even if the product is good.

**Business implication:** Listing optimization should be a core service component, not an optional add-on.

---

### Insight 3: Founders want clarity, not just activity

D2C founders do not only need someone to “manage the account.” They need to know what changed, what improved, which SKUs are performing, where money is being wasted, and what the next actions are.

**Business implication:** Monthly reporting should include SKU-level performance, traffic, conversion, ad spend, returns, account health, and next-step recommendations.

---

### Insight 4: Ad spend without structure creates distrust

Many brands are willing to spend on marketplace ads, but they lose confidence when they cannot understand ACOS, ROAS, campaign structure, or whether ads are helping overall sales.

**Business implication:** Aneeverse should position ad coordination/reporting as a key value driver, even if full ad management is priced separately.

---

### Insight 5: Jewellery and luggage need category-specific listing logic

Jewellery listings must build trust and visual appeal. Luggage listings must explain utility, size, durability, warranty, and feature comparison.

**Business implication:** Aneeverse should use category-specific listing templates instead of generic product upload formats.

# 3. Pricing & Revenue Model

## 3.1 Pricing Strategy Recommendation

Aneeverse should avoid very cheap pricing because marketplace management is operationally heavy. Pricing should be based on scope, number of SKUs, number of marketplaces, reporting depth, and ad involvement.

The recommended model is a **monthly retainer + optional performance incentive + setup fee**.

---

## 3.2 Recommended Packages

### Package 1: Marketplace Launch Setup

**Best for:** Brands entering marketplaces for the first time  
**Suggested Price:** ₹15,000 – ₹35,000 one-time setup fee  

Includes:

- Seller account setup support
- Documentation checklist
- Marketplace onboarding guidance
- Initial catalogue upload
- Basic listing optimization
- Title, bullet, and description structure
- Basic keyword research
- Brand store setup where applicable

---

### Package 2: Monthly Marketplace Management

**Best for:** Brands already live but not growing consistently  
**Suggested Price:** ₹25,000 – ₹60,000/month  

Includes:

- Listing optimization
- Catalogue maintenance
- Account health monitoring
- Inventory coordination
- Offer and deal planning
- Review/rating monitoring
- Marketplace issue tracking
- Monthly performance reporting

---

### Package 3: Growth + Ads Coordination

**Best for:** Brands serious about scaling marketplace sales  
**Suggested Price:** ₹60,000 – ₹1,25,000/month  

Includes:

- Everything in monthly management
- SKU-level growth planning
- Hero product strategy
- Ad campaign coordination
- Keyword and competitor tracking
- Sale-event planning
- Revenue and ROAS tracking
- Monthly growth review call

Optional performance incentive:

- 3%–7% of incremental marketplace revenue above agreed baseline
- Or fixed milestone bonus after hitting revenue targets

## 3.4 Learnings, Results, and Next Steps

### Current Learnings

Based on the current service analysis:

1. One-time setup pricing is useful for entry-level clients but does not create stable recurring revenue.
2. Monthly retainer is the strongest base revenue model because marketplace management requires continuous execution.
3. Performance-only pricing is risky because marketplace sales depend on client-side inventory, pricing, product quality, reviews, and dispatch discipline.
4. Hybrid pricing is the best model for serious brands: fixed monthly retainer + optional performance bonus.
5. Pricing must increase when SKU count, marketplace count, or ad complexity increases.

### Results So Far

Formal close-rate, objection, and accepted-price data is not yet available. This should be tracked from upcoming sales calls.

### Next Steps

1. Speak to 10–15 D2C founders/ecommerce heads.
2. Test the three package options in real sales conversations.
3. Track objections and willingness to pay.

5. Choose one default package to push for the next 30 days.

## 3.5 Pricing Strategy Revisions

### Initial Pricing Assumption

Initial assumption: offer marketplace management as a simple monthly service.

### Revised Pricing Strategy

The better strategy is a tiered model:

1. **Setup Fee** for onboarding and initial catalogue work
2. **Monthly Retainer** for ongoing operations and growth management
3. **Optional Performance Incentive** for serious scale clients

### Reason for Revision

A flat monthly fee does not account for differences in SKU count, marketplace complexity, ad involvement, or operational load. A tiered model makes pricing more flexible and protects margins.
